Why this location deserves a proper second look

Am Kissel is not just a club for Königswinter. It sits in the Rhine valley beside the B42 and station area, with Bad Honnef to the south, southern Bonn across the river, and the wider Rhein-Sieg commuter belt behind it.

Four overlapping markets

Königswinter / Siebengebirge
Immediate residents, families and local racket-sport users.

Bad Honnef / B42 south
Affluent valley population on a direct road corridor.

Bonn-Bad Godesberg
Secondary west-bank market supported by two ferries.

Sankt Augustin / Hennef
Broader regional and commuter demand.

Underwriting rule

The right bank should carry the base business case. Bonn demand reached via ferry is meaningful upside, but the project should not require a ferry crossing to work.

That gives us a more resilient thesis against ferry schedules, low-water disruption and peak-hour waiting time.

Demographic fit is different from Bonn

The immediate market is older and affluent. As the catchment widens, it becomes younger and more family-heavy. That argues for padel as the anchor, with Reformer, academy and active-health programming doing more commercial work than a dedicated yoga business.

Commercial message

“Play on your way home.” The best public site-adjacent B42 figure found in this first pass is roughly 27,000 vehicles/day. That should be refreshed before financing use, but it is enough to justify treating visibility and after-work convenience as major site assets.

Three concepts to test with the landowner

The purpose now is not final architecture. We want to show credible use of the land, then discover what the owner will contribute to grading, groundworks, access and permanent infrastructure.

CONCEPT A

7-court flagship

Seven indoor courts, compact lounge/café, academy, 8 reformers, flexible studio and 35–45 parking spaces.

CONCEPT B

Active lifestyle

Six courts, 10–12 reformers, larger studio and stronger health/family programming. Potentially the strongest demographic fit.

CONCEPT C

Expandable

Six courts on day one with structural reserve for court seven. Lower initial risk while retaining scale.

Landowner discussion: what should stay with the real estate

The Much strategy gets materially stronger if permanent site works are carried or co-funded by the property side while Padel Jungle carries the operating and movable assets.

Ask the owner to cover / co-fund

  • Erdarbeiten and grading
  • ground improvement, compaction and hall sub-base
  • drainage / storm-water solution
  • utilities to the building edge
  • permanent access / internal road
  • parking sub-base or paving contribution
  • retaining or slope works if required
  • planning cooperation, easements and parcel consolidation

Why this split is logical

These works improve the owner's land regardless of the tenant. Courts, modular hall components, fit-out and club equipment are more directly tied to the operating business.

Main objective of the next owner meeting: define the feasible footprint and discover how much permanent infrastructure they are prepared to deliver.

Current decision view

The market case is strong enough to justify the next stage. The key unknowns are now physical feasibility, planning rights and the landowner's contribution.

What supports the site

Large 20–30 minute catchment · high purchasing power · B42 visibility · strong Bonn commuter relationship · two Rhine ferries · family/school demand · unusually good fit for Reformer and daytime programming.

What needs solving next

Current cadastral data · exact usable area · sport/leisure permissibility · height/GRZ/GFZ/BMZ · B42 setback/consent · topography/geotechnics · access/parking · noise · owner contribution · land terms.
